# Key Business Findings

## 1. Overview

This report summarises the main findings from the LendingClub Credit Risk Analysis project. The analysis explores how borrower and loan characteristics relate to observed loan performance.

The findings are based on 1,306,387 classified loans.

- Good loans: 1,043,940
- Bad loans: 262,447
- Overall bad-loan rate: 20.07%

## 2. Key Findings

### Loan Grade

Bad-loan rates increased across the loan-grade categories.

- Grade A recorded a bad-loan rate of 6.09%.
- Grade G recorded a bad-loan rate of 49.80%.

This indicates substantial differences in observed loan performance across grades.

### Debt-to-Income Ratio (DTI)

- Loans with DTI above 40 recorded a bad-loan rate of 31.78%.
- Loans with DTI between 0 and 10 recorded a rate of 14.92%.

Higher DTI bands were generally associated with higher observed bad-loan rates.

### Loan Term

- 36-month loans: 16.12%.
- 60-month loans: 32.56%.

The observed bad-loan rate was higher among loans with a 60-month term.

### Annual Income

- Annual income below $25,000: 24.87%.
- Annual income above $150,000: 15.06%.

Lower-income borrowers showed a higher observed bad-loan rate in this analysis.

### Loan Amount

- Loans below $5,000: 15.69%.
- Loans above $35,000: 23.92%.

Higher loan amounts were associated with higher observed bad-loan rates in these segments.

### Home Ownership

- Renters: 23.36%.
- Mortgage holders: 17.32%.

Renters recorded a higher observed bad-loan rate than borrowers with mortgages.

### Loan Purpose

- Small business loans: 29.91%.
- Debt consolidation loans: 21.27%.

Small business loans showed a higher observed bad-loan rate in the analysed population.

### Income Verification

- Verified income: 23.94%.
- Not verified income: 14.77%.

Verified-income loans had a higher observed bad-loan rate. This is an association and should not be interpreted as evidence that verification increases risk.

## 3. Business Implications

The findings suggest several areas worth considering in portfolio monitoring:

- Monitor loan-grade performance to identify segments with elevated observed risk.
- Consider DTI alongside other borrower affordability indicators.
- Compare repayment-term performance when reviewing portfolio composition.
- Monitor loan purposes and loan amounts for differences in observed performance.
- Avoid relying on any single characteristic when assessing borrower risk.

## 4. Important Interpretation Notes

These findings describe historical patterns and associations within the classified loan population. They do not establish causation or constitute a validated predictive credit-scoring model.

Unresolved loan statuses were excluded from the binary good/bad outcome analysis.

## 5. Conclusion

The analysis identified meaningful differences in observed bad-loan rates across loan grades, DTI bands, income groups, loan terms, loan amounts, home ownership, loan purposes and income-verification categories.

These findings demonstrate how exploratory data analysis can support more informed credit-risk monitoring and portfolio review.
